Launch into Computing

Module Overview

This module introduced me to the foundations of computing, covering software engineering, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and data science. Building on my experience in banking technology, I focused particularly on the role of AI in addressing business challenges and the ethical considerations surrounding its adoption. The assessments also gave me an opportunity to explore programming in Python and JavaScript, while considering the importance of responsible software development, including the legal, ethical and professional responsibilities of computing practitioners.

Learning Outcomes

The module was structured around four learning outcomes, covering the foundations of computing, its practical applications in enterprise environments, and the ethical, legal and professional challenges associated with emerging technologies.

  1. Identify and critically analyse computing challenges and processes in business systems, accounting for the current enterprise landscape.
  2. Gather and synthesise information from multiple sources (including internet resources and business publications) to aid in the systematic design and analysis of computing challenges.
  3. Critically evaluate appropriate methodologies, tools and techniques to mitigate and/or solve computing issues and their business impact.
  4. Articulate the legal, social, ethical, and professional issues faced by computing professionals.

Module Reflection

Launch into Computing provided me with a broader understanding of the different disciplines within computing, including software engineering, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and data science. Although the module covered several areas, I decided to focus primarily on artificial intelligence again, as this was the main reason I had chosen to return to postgraduate study. Given my professional background in banking technology, I was particularly interested in exploring how AI could be applied within an enterprise environment, as well as the ethical and professional responsibilities associated with its adoption.

Balancing the module alongside full-time work was challenging at times. I was only able to attend one seminar live due to work commitments and relied on the recordings for the remaining sessions. Although this allowed me to study at a time that suited me, I missed the opportunity to participate more actively in discussions. I also made a conscious decision not to complete the formative Python exercises, mainly because I wanted to prioritise the assessed work. I knew that I would eventually have to develop my programming skills as I progressed through the programme, so this was more a case of postponing the learning rather than avoiding it altogether. Looking back, I recognise that completing these exercises would probably have made some of the practical elements of the module easier.

Of the two individual end of module assignments, I was surprised to find the programming exercise and comparison of Python and JavaScript considerably easier than the recorded presentation. I had initially expected the programming assignment to be more challenging, particularly given my limited practical coding experience. However, I enjoyed examining how both languages approached the same problem and explaining the differences in their syntax, readability and error handling. It was also interesting to consider the ethical implications of what appeared to be a relatively straightforward programming task, particularly how decisions made during development could affect the accuracy and reliability of the results.

The recorded presentation was a different experience and probably the most frustrating aspect of the module. This came as something of a surprise, as presenting is a regular part of my professional role, and I am comfortable communicating complex information to colleagues and senior stakeholders. However, I quickly discovered that delivering a recorded academic presentation was quite different from presenting at work. I was not accustomed to scripting my presentations in such detail, and having to balance a carefully prepared script with a natural delivery was more difficult than I had anticipated. The limited recording time added another challenge, as I wanted to explore the topic in sufficient depth without rushing or sounding as though I was simply reading from a script. I ended up having to significantly edit and re-record sections to improve the flow and remain within the time limit. Given how familiar I am with presenting professionally, I had not expected this assessment to require so much effort. It did, however, make me appreciate the differences between workplace presentations and academic communication, particularly the importance of structuring arguments carefully and being selective about what to include.

Reflecting on the module, I am pleased that I chose to concentrate on artificial intelligence, as it allowed me to explore the subject from a slightly different perspective and build on my existing professional experience. At the same time, the module reminded me that developing a broader understanding of computing is important, particularly as AI solutions depend on areas such as software engineering, data management and cybersecurity. I also recognise that my practical programming skills remain an area for development, especially having chosen not to complete the formative exercises. Overall, the module reinforced my interest in AI and its potential applications within financial services, while also giving me a better appreciation of the technical, ethical and communication skills I will need to develop throughout the remainder of the programme.

Reflection Notes

What Went Well

Finding the Python and JavaScript programming comparison more straightforward than expected, particularly the code explanation and ethical considerations.

Biggest Challenge

Balancing the module alongside full-time work and adapting to the demands of a tightly scripted, timed academic presentation despite being accustomed to presenting professionally.

Area for Development

Developing my practical Python programming skills and becoming more confident delivering concise, natural recorded presentations.

Key Takeaway

Effective computing requires more than technical knowledge. Ethical decision-making, clear communication and an understanding of the wider business context are equally important.
