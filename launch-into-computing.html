Launch into Computing

Module Overview

This module introduced me to several of the main disciplines within computing, including software engineering, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, data science and enterprise IT. I was particularly interested in artificial intelligence and the ethical considerations involved in software development. Through the collaborative discussion and individual assessments, I explored how computing has evolved, compared programming approaches and considered how organisations can adopt emerging technologies responsibly.

Selected project

Ethical Programming Exercise

Developed and compared Python and JavaScript solutions to remove duplicate entries while preserving the original capitalisation of the first occurrence, and considered the ethical implications of data processing decisions.

Python · JavaScript · Data quality and ethics

Learning Outcomes

The module covered the foundations of computing, practical problem-solving and the wider professional responsibilities associated with technology.

  1. Examine the main computing disciplines and understand how they contribute to modern organisations and technological change.
  2. Develop an understanding of programming concepts, algorithms and approaches to solving practical problems.
  3. Compare programming languages and reflect on the strengths and limitations of different implementations.
  4. Consider the ethical, legal and professional responsibilities involved in computing and the adoption of emerging technologies.

Module Reflection

Launch into Computing gave me an opportunity to look beyond artificial intelligence and consider some of the wider disciplines that underpin modern computing. Although I was already familiar with many of these areas through my work in banking technology, the module encouraged me to think more carefully about the underlying concepts and how they relate to one another.

I found the programming exercise particularly useful. Writing solutions in both Python and JavaScript made me think about how the same problem can be approached in different ways. The exercise itself was relatively straightforward, but considering readability, error handling and the implications of removing duplicate data showed me that even a small programming task involves decisions that are not purely technical.

For the individual presentation, I chose to focus on artificial intelligence in the enterprise. This was an opportunity to draw on my professional experience while exploring some of the governance issues that organisations need to address when adopting AI, particularly generative AI. It reinforced my view that the technology itself is only part of the challenge; organisations also need appropriate controls, clear accountability and a realistic understanding of its limitations.

Looking back, the module helped me become more comfortable with practical programming and more conscious of the ethical decisions involved in technical work. I still want to develop my programming skills further, but I now have a stronger foundation to build on as I progress through the programme.

Reflection Notes

What Went Well

Applying my banking technology experience to discussions about enterprise AI and its wider implications.

Moving from conceptual discussions to writing, testing and comparing working solutions in two programming languages.

Key Takeaway

Even relatively simple coding decisions can have implications for data quality, fairness and responsible technology use.
