Understanding Artificial Intelligence

University of Essex Online 12-week module Distinction

Module Overview

This module introduced me to the principles and practical applications of artificial intelligence. Building on my experience in banking technology, I focused particularly on how machine learning could support financial services, from credit risk assessment to fraud detection. The assessments also gave me an opportunity to experiment with machine learning models using WEKA and consider the challenges of applying AI in a regulated environment.

Applied project

Credit risk assessment

Compared decision trees, Random Forest and AdaBoost using WEKA to explore how machine learning models could be used to assess credit default risk.

30,000 customer records · Model evaluation · Explainability and fairness

Learning Outcomes

The module was structured around four learning outcomes, covering the principles, application and wider implications of AI.

  1. Understand the legal, ethical and professional issues brought up by AI and the impact of AI on society.
  2. Understand and critically analyse the essential concepts, principles, methods, techniques and problems of AI.
  3. Demonstrate a critical understanding of data requirements and programming paradigms applicable to AI.
  4. Apply and evaluate critically the various methods, tools and technologies applied to an AI project in order to develop an effective plan and delivery of solutions to a business problem.

Module Reflection

This module helped me develop a much better understanding of artificial intelligence, particularly how machine learning can be applied to real-world business challenges. I found the focus on financial services especially interesting, as it allowed me to draw on my professional experience in banking technology and core banking transformation.

Balancing the module alongside full-time work was not always straightforward. I was never able to attend the seminars live and instead relied entirely on watching the recordings afterwards. While this gave me some flexibility, I did miss the opportunity to participate in discussions and ask questions as they arose. I also made a conscious decision to skip the Python exercises, partly because of time constraints and knowing that I would eventually need to develop these skills as part of the wider programme. Looking back, I probably would have benefited from spending more time on them, particularly as I became more familiar with the practical aspects of machine learning.

I also found the assignments quite challenging initially. Having not undertaken any academic writing for approximately ten years, it took some time to get back into the habit of researching, structuring arguments and referencing academic sources. I was therefore particularly pleased with my results, ultimately achieving a distinction in the module overall. This gave me more confidence in my ability to return to postgraduate study after such a long break.

The AI Solution Implementation assessment was probably the most interesting part of the module, although it was also one of the more frustrating at times. I enjoyed experimenting with different machine learning algorithms in WEKA, but initially struggled with configuring some of the parameters. At one point, I incorrectly adjusted the settings for AdaBoost, which resulted in the model taking over an hour to run. Experiences like this helped me appreciate how much difference seemingly small configuration changes can make. More importantly, comparing different models for credit risk assessment reinforced that choosing an algorithm is not simply about achieving the highest accuracy. Explainability, fairness and regulatory considerations are equally important, particularly in banking.

This module has given me a stronger foundation in AI and a better appreciation of the challenges involved in applying it within a business environment. It has also highlighted areas where I need to develop further, particularly my practical programming skills. One of my main takeaways is that successfully implementing AI involves much more than selecting the right technology; it also requires appropriate governance, good-quality data and a clear understanding of the business problem being addressed. These are areas I am keen to explore further as I progress through the programme, particularly given my interest in how AI can support banking modernisation, risk management and operational efficiency.
